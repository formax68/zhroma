<objective>
Bring the three committed fixtures to the current sanitizer contract, so that the
manifest's provenance claim becomes mechanically checkable and the corpus can be
enforced against the shared grammar in Plan 01-13.

Purpose: `01-VERIFICATION.md` records that every committed fixture is rejected by
the current sanitizer with `aria-attribute-invalid` — the corpus and the contract
that supposedly produced it have diverged. A planning-time probe established the
exact scope of that divergence and one further fact the review did not state:

1. Twenty enum-valued ARIA attributes across the three files hold a redaction
   stand-in rather than a value from their enum — 12 `aria-autocomplete` in the
   grouped-long file, 4 `aria-haspopup` in the absence control, 4
   `aria-autocomplete` in the canonical file. An earlier projection wrote the
   stand-in before the sanitizer classified ARIA attributes by type. Because an
   enum value that was never recorded cannot be recovered, the only honest
   sanitizer-consistent treatment is to drop those attributes, exactly as the
   sanitizer drops other non-selector-relevant attributes.
2. Every header cell in all three files holds a `TEXT-NNN` stand-in, so no header
   cell reads the Priority token. With Plan 01-11's header-token preservation in
   place, restoring that exact token at zero-based header index 6 in the two
   Priority-present fixtures makes re-sanitization byte-stable and is faithful to
   the `priority-representation` ledger entry, which records Priority at index 6
   in both Priority-present scenarios and names that exact header as the only
   permitted Priority fallback.

With those two narrowing edits, the probe confirmed the absence control and the
grouped-long file already re-sanitize to byte-identical bytes.

Output: three re-admitted fixtures, an honest manifest, and a regression test that
keeps the corpus and the contract from diverging again.
</objective>
